# 1.6 plan: cast a virtual display to a Chromecast

Status: approved and implemented in 1.6.0-EN.

## Goal

Run an app on a virtual display at the resolution you choose (for example 1280×720) and show that display on a
Chromecast **1:1**, with the app's sound, while the tablet's own screen stays free. The tablet's screen resolution
plays no part.

## How it works

```
app on virtual display #2 (1280×720)
        │  frames (VirtualDisplay#setSurface, same switch as the eye)
        ▼
MoreDisplay app: frame hub (OpenGL) ──► eye window (optional, at the same time)
        │
        ▼
hardware H.264 encoder (1280×720, no scaling)      app's sound (Vector only, see Audio)
        │                                                 │ PCM via a pipe from system_server
        ▼                                                 ▼
             MPEG-TS muxer ──► 1-second HLS segments ──► small HTTP server on the tablet (Wi-Fi only)
                                                                │
                                Google Cast SDK: "play this live stream" ──► Chromecast (Default Media Receiver)
```

- The Chromecast plays the stream with its built-in player (Google's *Default Media Receiver*), so **no Google
  developer account or registration** is needed.
- Stream: H.264 video + AAC audio in MPEG-TS, HLS live playlist with 1-second segments and a short sliding window.
  The HTTP server sends the CORS headers the Chromecast player requires and serves only a random, per-session URL.
- Expected delay: **about 2–5 seconds**. Picture and sound are delayed together, so they stay in sync on the TV.

## Settings → Cast

| Setting | Default | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display to cast | none | Picked from the list of managed displays. Cast at its own resolution. |
| Video quality | Auto | Auto = 4 Mbit/s up to 720p, 8 Mbit/s up to 1080p. Manual 2–20 Mbit/s. |
| Frame rate | 30 fps | 30 or 60. |
| Cast the app's sound | on | Vector only. Takes the sound of the apps on that display off the tablet speaker and sends it to the TV. |
| Keep the tablet awake while casting | off | Only if testing shows that apps pause when the tablet screen turns off (see Risks). |

## Starting and stopping

- **Cast button** at the top of the Displays tab (the standard Chromecast icon). Tap it, pick a TV from the list of
  Cast devices on your Wi-Fi, and the display set in Settings starts casting.
- The display's card shows **Casting to Living Room TV** with a **Stop** button.
- A notification **Casting display #2 to Living Room TV** with **Stop**. Android requires this (a foreground
  service) to keep casting while MoreDisplay is in the background.
- adb: `--es action cast --es state on|off [--ei display_id N]` (uses the last Cast device).
- Casting stops when the display is removed, the TV is turned off or switches input, or Wi-Fi drops.

## Eye and cast together

While casting, the display's frames go to MoreDisplay's frame hub, which feeds the encoder and, when the eye is
on, the eye window, both at full quality. Without casting, the eye works exactly as in 1.4/1.5.

## Audio (Vector only)

- In system_server, MoreDisplay registers an Android audio routing rule for the uids of the apps on the cast
  display: their sound goes **only** into a capture stream (not the speaker), which is passed to the app through a
  pipe and encoded together with the picture. When casting stops, the rule is removed and the sound returns to the
  tablet.
- Apps that forbid capturing their sound are respected: the TV gets no sound from them.
- With Shizuku, casting is video-only (the shell has no permission to reroute audio).

## Limits

- **Copy-protected video stays black** (Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video and similar mark their video as protected,
  and Android won't put it on a virtual display). This is intentional and won't be worked around; use those apps'
  own Cast button.
- Apps that forbid sound capture: picture without sound.
- Needs Google Play services on the tablet, and the tablet and Chromecast on the same Wi-Fi.
- One cast at a time. Delay about 2–5 s (a low-delay version would need a registered custom receiver: possible later).

## Risks to check on the device

1. **Tablet screen off**: on some Android versions, apps on all displays pause when the main screen turns off. If
   that happens on your tablet, the "Keep the tablet awake while casting" option keeps it awake at minimum brightness.
2. **Chromecast loading the stream from the tablet over plain HTTP on the local network**: works for local-casting
   apps (with CORS headers); confirmed only by your test.
3. Hardware encoder limits on older devices (1080p60 may be too much; 720p30 is safe).
